Quantum responses: limits and independence

Continuous responses can approach a jump.

Consider one quantum spin with measured outcomes −1 and +1. In a simple thermal model, their weights are proportional to exp[−β(h − a)] and exp[β(h − a)], respectively. Here h is a field parameter expressed in energy units, a is an offset, and β is the inverse thermal energy. The mean response is

mβ,a(h) = tanh[β(h − a)].

Cooling increases β. Every finite-temperature curve is continuous, but its pointwise limit is −1 below a, +1 above a, and zero at a. At fixed β, varying a gives a pointwise ordered family. The witness is a common field value h. Three ordered curves realize at most four low–high prescriptions at fixed separated thresholds, whereas independence would require all eight.

The course develops the many-spin setting through thermodynamic limits and studies independence for families of quantum responses.

Where the route arrives

Model theory: the common language.

Based on the book by {{ course.authors | escape }}.

Every translation on this page reads one configuration: the instances of a single formula φ(x, y) whose truth values can be prescribed independently. Shelah isolated this configuration in model theory as the independence property. Its absence, NIP, is the dividing line that the course follows through each subject.

Model theory supplies the grammar that the translations share. It explains why one restriction governs pointwise closures, VC classes, ℓ₁ sequences, tame dynamics, and quantum responses. We develop each subject in its own language and then return to model theory, where the readings become instances of one property of a formula.

The main development assumes no previous course in model theory. Optional readings make the logical connections precise for students familiar with compactness, types, and saturation.
