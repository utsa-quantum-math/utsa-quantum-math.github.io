We characterize simulation of a general probabilistic system of countable dimension by the countable classical target of Catani, Galley and Gonda: a simulation exists exactly when finite partial simulations can be extended coherently, without revising the masses and responses already assigned to finitely many ontic states. A transfinite derivative of these partial simulations ranks the failures of countable simulation; its first level recovers the countably additive excess of our earlier paper, and its fixed point decides, at each error, whether a countable simulation exists. On Cantor space we exhibit a system with countable simulations of every positive error and no exact one, and show that two demands defeat every exact simulation through a finite prepare-and-measure test chosen after the model has committed itself. We translate this defeat into a winning strategy in the refutation game of Aubone and Iovino for real-valued infinitary logic.

This paper is the sequel to [Compactness and the classical excess](/resources/compactness-classical-excess/).
