We show that two successive demands rule out exact countable classical simulation of the Lipschitz Cantor system introduced in Part I, even though every numerical level of the hierarchy developed here is zero. The first demand asks the modeler to place most of one preparation's mass on finitely many ontic states. The answer determines a finite prepare-and-measure test that rules out exact completion. For a system of countable dimension, a countable classical simulation at a prescribed error exists exactly when there is a nonempty family of finite partial simulations in which each member can be extended within the family to include any prescribed stage and leave arbitrarily little preparation mass unassigned while preserving the masses and responses already assigned. A transfinite derivative successively removes commitments that fail this extension test. Its first level recovers the countably additive classical excess, and the commitments at its fixed point are exactly the restrictions of countable simulations at the prescribed error. We translate the two-demand obstruction into a winning strategy in the refutation game of Aubone and Iovino for real-valued infinitary logic.
