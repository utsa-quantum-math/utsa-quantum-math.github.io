In this paper we characterize the simulations of a general probabilistic system of countable dimension by the countable classical target of Catani, Galley and Gonda, a target for which finite feasibility no longer suffices: a system whose finite parts are all simulated exactly can have classical excess 1. The missing condition is coherent extension: a simulation by the countable target must extend each of its finite parts without revising the masses and responses already assigned to finitely many ontic states, a requirement that compactness, which assembles finite simulations chosen independently of one another, cannot see. We characterize the classical excess by consistency properties of finite partial simulations, the device that takes the place of compactness in infinitary logic. A transfinite derivative of these properties ranks the ways in which a countable simulation fails: the empty partial simulation survives its first level exactly at errors at least the countably additive excess, and its fixed point decides, at each prescribed error, whether a countable simulation exists. The central example is a system on Cantor space that has countable simulations of every positive error and no exact one: every level of its excess vanishes, yet two demands defeat every exact countable simulation. The numerical excess records that the system can be simulated as accurately as one wishes; the rank records how an exact simulation fails, and locates the failure in a finite prepare-and-measure test chosen after the model has committed itself.
