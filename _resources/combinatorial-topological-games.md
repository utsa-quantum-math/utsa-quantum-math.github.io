Part I (Topological games and Baire spaces) proves that a space is a Baire space exactly when the first player has no winning strategy in the Banach–Mazur game, and that a metrizable space is completely metrizable exactly when the second player has a winning strategy in the strong Choquet game, with a full proof of the hard direction. Parts II and III collect applications of the Banach–Mazur and Choquet games and of other topological games, Part IV tells the story of the Scottish Book, and an appendix treats three non-topological games. This document merges the former notes "Topological Games and Baire Spaces" and "Applications of Combinatorial and Topological Games in Classical Mathematics". The last update is printed on the upper left corner of the cover page.
