We prove that a general probabilistic system has a countably additive classical simulation at a prescribed error as soon as each of its finitely generated subsystems has a finite classical simulation at that error. Consequently, whenever an error bound is infeasible for countably additive classical simulation, finitely many preparations and effects already witness the infeasibility. The countably additive classical excess is attained and equals the supremum of the classical excesses of the finitely generated subsystems. For bounded finite-dimensional systems we show that the classical excess of Catani, Galley and Gonda is attained on a number of sample points bounded by the dimensions, which makes their stabilization criterion unconditional. We show that both conclusions fail, and fail independently, when the model is required to have countably many ontic states. On Cantor space, one system has classical excess 1 for the countable target although every finitely generated subsystem has excess 0, and another has excess 0 for that target but no exact countable simulation.
