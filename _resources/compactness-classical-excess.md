We prove that a general probabilistic system has a countably additive classical simulation at a prescribed error as soon as each of its finitely generated subsystems has a finite classical simulation at that error. Consequently, whenever a prescribed error bound is infeasible for a countably additive classical simulation, finitely many preparations and effects already witness its infeasibility. The countably additive classical excess is attained and equals the supremum of the classical excesses of the finitely generated subsystems. The key is compactness: an ultraproduct of the finite models, followed by Loeb's construction, gives one model that meets the bound on the whole system. For bounded finite-dimensional systems we show that the classical excess of Catani, Galley and Gonda is attained on a number of sample points bounded by the dimensions, which makes their stabilization criterion unconditional. Two Cantor-space systems show what changes when the model is required to have countably many ontic states. In one, the countable excess is one although every finitely generated subsystem has excess zero. In the other, the countable excess is zero but no exact countable simulation exists.
