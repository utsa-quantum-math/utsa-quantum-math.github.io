We prove that a general probabilistic system has a countably additive classical simulation at a prescribed error as soon as each of its finitely generated subsystems has a finite classical simulation at that error, so that the countably additive classical excess is attained and is determined by the finitely generated subsystems. The key is compactness: an ultraproduct of the finite models, followed by Loeb's construction, gives one model that meets the bound on the whole system. For bounded finite-dimensional systems we show that the classical excess of Catani, Galley and Gonda is attained on a number of sample points bounded by the dimensions, which makes their stabilization criterion unconditional. Two systems on Cantor space show that for the countable target both conclusions fail, and fail independently.
