The countably additive classical excess of a general probabilistic system is determined by its finitely generated subsystems. If every such subsystem has a finite classical simulation at a prescribed error, one countably additive model meets that error bound on the whole system. An ultraproduct of finite classical models followed by Loeb's construction gives a model that attains the infimum. For systems with bounded finite-dimensional state and effect spaces, exact compression also gives an optimal model for the original classical excess on finitely many sample points, with a bound in terms of the affine dimension of the state space and the dimension of the linear span of the effects. Two examples on Cantor space show that finite determination and attainment can fail independently for the countable target.
