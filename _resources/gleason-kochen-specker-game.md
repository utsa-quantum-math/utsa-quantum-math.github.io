We axiomatize countably additive quantum probability and prove a completeness theorem for finite refutation beyond compactness. A countable theory of closed conditions in our real-valued infinitary logic, each bounding the value of a sentence by a rational number, has no model exactly when a challenger can force a finite contradiction in a game, whatever bounds the model builder proposes. The tree of every winning strategy is well-founded, with finitely many incompatible commitments at each leaf. Our axioms describe, in their standard models, the closed subspaces and the density-operator probabilities of a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space. Adjoining predetermined, context-independent outcomes gives a theory with no models, standard or not, although every finite subset of its axioms has a model. Every winning strategy for this theory must branch infinitely somewhere. In our strategy, countable additivity identifies the four-dimensional subspace in which to apply a finite Kochen–Specker parity argument. We also prove in ZFC that the sets of probability values definable uniformly across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval.
