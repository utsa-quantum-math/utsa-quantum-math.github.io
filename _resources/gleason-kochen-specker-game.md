The Kochen–Specker theorem rules out hidden variables by exhibiting finitely many vectors in a Hilbert space to which the values 0 and 1 cannot be assigned consistently. We show that this impossibility does not need the Hilbert space itself: it follows from countably many axioms of real-valued infinitary logic and therefore holds in every model of them, including countable models that are not Hilbert spaces. Turning the model existence theorem into a game, we show that it is still witnessed by finite contradictions, with infinity entering only through countable sums. This changes what the theorem is about: it is usually read as a fact about the geometry of a particular Hilbert space, resting on its completeness and its continuum of vectors, whereas here hidden variables are excluded by the logical structure of quantum propositions and their probabilities alone. The game also explains why finite configurations, such as those of Kochen and Specker, are always enough to finish a refutation.
