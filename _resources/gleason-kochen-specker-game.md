Kochen and Specker excluded noncontextual hidden variables by finite configurations of rays. J. L. Bell expressed their certificates in propositional logic, where compactness turns every inconsistency into an inconsistent finite set, and Hrushovski and Pitowsky obtained quantitative information about quantum probability from a first-order formulation in finite dimension. In infinitary real-valued logic, the infinitary form of Keisler’s model theory for real-valued structures, we prove that every obstruction to noncontextual hidden variables is finite in the full setting of quantum mechanics.

We axiomatize quantum probability by a theory whose standard models represent exactly the density-operator measures on the closed-subspace lattices of separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces, the setting of Gleason’s theorem. We then prove a complete refutation theorem: a countable set of closed conditions in this logic is inconsistent exactly when a well-founded tree whose branches end in finite contradictions refutes it. Every hidden-variable hypothesis given by closed conditions and inconsistent with the axioms of quantum probability is therefore refuted by finite contradictions. The finiteness does not come from compactness. Adjoining the dispersion-free hypothesis gives an inconsistent theory every finite subset of which is consistent, and every refutation of the theory branches infinitely. In the refutation we construct, countable additivity leads the hypothetical measure to select a four-dimensional subspace in which a parity argument yields the contradiction, and the refutation records separately the geometric identities and each use of noncontextuality. The same language defines, uniformly over the standard models, exactly the σ-projective sets of probabilities. All these results are proved in ZFC.
