We axiomatize countably additive quantum probability and prove a completeness theorem for finite refutation beyond compactness. Adjoining the hypothesis that every event has a predetermined, context-independent outcome makes the theory inconsistent, although every finite subset of the resulting axioms has a model. For a countable theory of closed bounds on sentence values in our real-valued infinitary logic, inconsistency is equivalent to a winning strategy for a challenger in a game against a model builder. The challenger chooses each test in response to the builder's commitments, and every play that follows a winning strategy ends with finitely many commitments that no structure satisfies together. For the quantum theory with predetermined outcomes, every winning strategy must branch infinitely at some node. In the strategy we construct, countable additivity locates the four-dimensional subspace in which a finite Kochen–Specker parity argument applies. In their standard models, our axioms describe the closed subspaces and the density-operator probabilities of a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space. We prove in ZFC that the sets of probability values definable uniformly across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval.
