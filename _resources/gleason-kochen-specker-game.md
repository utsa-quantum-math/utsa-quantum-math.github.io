We give a new axiomatic framework for countably additive quantum probability and prove a completeness theorem for the refutation of hidden-variable hypotheses. In the standard models, those built on full Hilbert spaces, our real-valued infinitary axioms describe the lattice of closed subspaces of a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space and exactly the density-operator measures of Gleason’s theorem. Refutations are strategies in a game, in which a challenger tests a proposed model by asking its builder for bounds on the quantities named by the theory. A countable theory of closed conditions, non-strict inequalities between the values of sentences and rational numbers, has no model exactly when the challenger has a strategy that forces a finite contradiction, whichever answers the builder gives. This characterizes which hypotheses given by countably many closed conditions are incompatible with the axioms. Adjoining the hypothesis of predetermined, context-independent outcomes to the axioms gives a theory with no models, by a parity argument independent of Gleason’s theorem, yet every finite subset has a model. In the refutation we construct, countable additivity determines where the challenger places a finite Kochen–Specker configuration. Every winning strategy for this combined theory branches infinitely at some node. We also prove in ZFC that the sets of probability values definable uniformly across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval.
