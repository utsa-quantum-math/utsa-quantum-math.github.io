We study the obstructions to noncontextual hidden variables when quantum probability is described in full, by the complete lattice of closed subspaces of a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space together with its countably additive measures. We give such a description in infinitary real-valued logic, the infinitary extension of Keisler’s model theory of general structures, whose countable suprema and infima express the completeness of the lattice and the countable additivity of the measures. The standard models of our axioms, those built on full Hilbert spaces, represent exactly the density-operator measures of Gleason’s theorem. The infinitary sentences come at the price of compactness. The hypothesis that at each hidden state every event receives the value 0 or 1, whatever the context of measurement, contradicts the axioms, yet it is consistent with every finite subset of them. The obstructions nevertheless remain finite. We prove that in this logic every inconsistent countable set of closed conditions is refuted by a well-founded tree whose branches end in finite contradictions; for the hypothesis above, every such tree has an infinitely branching node. The resulting finiteness theorem is maximal: a hidden-variable hypothesis expressed by countably many closed conditions has no model of the axioms, standard or not, precisely when such a tree refutes it together with them. The two theorems play complementary roles, the first identifying the standard models with the quantum states and the second certifying every hypothesis that fails in all models. The same language defines, uniformly across standard models, exactly the σ-projective sets of probabilities, and all these results are proved in ZFC.
