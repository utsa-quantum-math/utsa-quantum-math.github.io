## What countable additivity contributes

A Kochen–Specker configuration refutes predetermined, context-independent outcomes with finitely many measurement events chosen in advance. In a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space with countably additive probability, the same hypothesis has no models, standard or not, and the proof does not use Gleason's theorem. Yet every finite subset of the axioms has a model, so no configuration fixed in advance refutes it. The refutation is instead a strategy in a game, in which a challenger tests a model builder's commitments about the values of events. Countable additivity lets the challenger locate, from the builder's own answers, a four-dimensional subspace in which the parity argument on the eighteen rays of Cabello, Estebaranz and García-Alcaine applies. This complements the fixed obstruction that finite additivity already gives on the full projection lattice, through Alda's projections of infinite rank and infinite corank.

## What remains finite when compactness fails

For countable theories of closed conditions in real-valued infinitary logic, we prove that a theory has no model exactly when the challenger has a winning strategy. The theorem builds on the model existence theorem of Aubone, Dueñez and Iovino. Every play that follows a winning strategy ends with finitely many commitments that no structure satisfies together, although the sentences involved may be infinitary. The tree of plays can branch infinitely only where the builder answers a challenge about a countable supremum or infimum, and for the quantum theory with predetermined outcomes every winning strategy must branch infinitely at some node.

## What the axioms recover and what they can define

In their standard models, the axioms recover the closed unit ball of a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, its lattice of closed subspaces, and the probabilities given by a unique density operator. The paper also gives an atom-based criterion for when the MacNeille completion of a pre-Solèr lattice is orthomodular. Finally, it proves in ZFC that the sets of probability values definable by a single formula uniformly across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval.

## Abstract

We axiomatize countably additive quantum probability and prove a completeness theorem for finite refutation beyond compactness: inconsistency is witnessed by refutations each of whose branches ends in finitely many jointly unsatisfiable commitments. Adjoining the hypothesis that every event has a predetermined, context-independent outcome makes the theory inconsistent, although every finite subset of the resulting axioms has a model. For a countable theory of closed bounds on sentence values in our real-valued infinitary logic, inconsistency is equivalent to a winning strategy for a challenger in a game against a model builder. The challenger chooses each test in response to the builder's commitments, and the plays that follow a winning strategy form such a refutation. For the quantum theory with predetermined outcomes, every winning strategy must branch infinitely at some node. In the strategy we construct, countable additivity locates the four-dimensional subspace in which a finite Kochen–Specker parity argument applies. In their standard models, our axioms describe the closed subspaces and the density-operator probabilities of a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space. We prove in ZFC that the sets of probability values definable uniformly across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval.
