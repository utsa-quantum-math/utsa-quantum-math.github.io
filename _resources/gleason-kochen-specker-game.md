We axiomatize countably additive quantum probability and prove a completeness theorem for finite refutation beyond compactness: inconsistency is witnessed by refutations each of whose branches ends in finitely many jointly unsatisfiable commitments. Adjoining the hypothesis that every event has a predetermined, context-independent outcome makes the theory inconsistent, although every finite subset of the resulting axioms has a model. For a countable theory of closed bounds on sentence values in our real-valued infinitary logic, inconsistency is equivalent to a winning strategy for a challenger in a game against a model builder. The challenger chooses each test in response to the builder's commitments, and the plays that follow a winning strategy form such a refutation. For the quantum theory with predetermined outcomes, every winning strategy must branch infinitely at some node. In the strategy we construct, countable additivity locates the four-dimensional subspace in which a finite parity argument of Kochen–Specker type applies. In their standard models, our axioms describe the closed subspaces and the density-operator probabilities of a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space. We prove in ZFC that the sets of probability values definable uniformly across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval.
