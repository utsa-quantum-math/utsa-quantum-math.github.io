Every obstruction to noncontextual hidden variables is finite, in infinite as in finite dimension. We prove this in infinitary real-valued logic, the infinitary extension of Keisler’s model theory of general structures, in which we axiomatize quantum probability in the framework of Gleason’s theorem. The standard models of our axioms, in which vectors and their square-summable sequences range over full closed unit balls of complex Hilbert spaces, represent exactly the density-operator measures on the closed subspaces of separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces. The finiteness comes from a refutation theorem: every inconsistent theory of closed conditions is refuted by a well-founded tree of finite contradictions. The result is maximal in a precise sense: every density-operator measure is represented by a standard model, and a hidden-variable hypothesis expressed by countably many closed conditions is inconsistent with the axioms if and only if finite contradictions refute the two together. The finiteness resides in the contradictions and cannot in general be transferred to the axioms. An outcome-deterministic noncontextual model would assign to every event the value 0 or 1 at each hidden state, whatever the context of measurement; with the axioms, this hypothesis yields a theory that is inconsistent yet finitely satisfiable, and every refutation of the theory branches infinitely. In the refutation we construct, the hypothetical measure selects, through countable additivity, the four-dimensional subspace in which a parity argument yields the contradiction. We also prove that the sets of probabilities uniformly definable across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval. All these results are proved in ZFC.
