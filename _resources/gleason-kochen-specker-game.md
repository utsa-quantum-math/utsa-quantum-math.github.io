We prove that every obstruction to noncontextual hidden variables in quantum mechanics is finite, including the obstructions that rest on Gleason's hypothesis of countable additivity. Since Kochen and Specker, such obstructions have been certified by finite configurations of rays, at the level of propositional logic: once a configuration is fixed, the obstruction is the inconsistency of finitely many Boolean constraints. Compactness, the source of these configurations, cannot reach the obstructions that rest on countable additivity: in infinite dimension the finite-dimensional events admit a finitely additive noncontextual valuation, and countable additivity constrains infinitely many events at once. The tool is a theorem of infinitary real-valued logic that takes the place of compactness: every countable theory of closed conditions without models is refuted by a well-founded tree of finite contradictions, given by the winning strategies of a model existence game. Quantum probability is axiomatized by such a theory, whose standard models are exactly those of Gleason's theorem. In the strategy we construct, a hypothetical noncontextual measure is not refuted by a configuration fixed in advance: through countable additivity, the measure itself selects the four-dimensional subspace in which the measure fails. The same logic defines, in the standard models, exactly the σ-projective sets of probabilities. Statements about σ-projective sets often go beyond ZFC, as the measurability of all these sets does; our theorems are nevertheless proved in ZFC, since the only determinacy they use is that of open games.
