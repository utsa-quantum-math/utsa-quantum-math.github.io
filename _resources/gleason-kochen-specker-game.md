The Kochen–Specker theorem shows that certain finite sets of rays in a Hilbert space admit no assignment of the values 0 and 1 compatible with orthogonality. We prove Gleason's theorem and a strengthening of the Kochen–Specker theorem in infinitary real-valued logic, in which the probability of a quantum proposition is the value of a formula. The hypothesis that every probability is 0 or 1 fails in every model, standard or not, of a countable theory whose standard models are the separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces with a density operator; in the standard models, so does every hypothesis that the probabilities avoid a nonempty Borel or σ-projective subset of [0,1]. In place of compactness we employ model-theoretic forcing, cast as a two-player topological game, which refutes each such hypothesis by finite obstructions that constrain the probabilities themselves, identify the axioms on which the refutation depends, and exist even when every finite set of axioms has a model.
