For sixty years the impossibility of noncontextual hidden variables has been certified by finite configurations, and the passage from Gleason's analytic theorem to finite obstructions has gone through compactness. We show that finite obstructions do not depend on compactness. Quantum probability on separable Hilbert space has a natural axiomatization in infinitary real-valued logic for which compactness fails: no finite set of axioms excludes noncontextual hidden variables, yet the whole theory excludes them in every model, standard or not. Finite obstructions exist nonetheless, for every refutation. They come from model existence rather than compactness, as the leaves of well-founded game trees, and they record separately the contexts, the geometric identities between events and the use of countable additivity, which the propositional encoding of contextuality merges. The same logic has exactly the expressive power of the σ-projective sets: these are precisely the sets of probabilities that its formulas define in the standard models, so that ZFC does not decide whether every such set is Lebesgue measurable, which fails in Gödel's constructible universe and holds under large cardinals.
