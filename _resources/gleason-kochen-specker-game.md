We prove that every obstruction to noncontextual hidden variables in quantum mechanics is finite, including the obstructions that rest on Gleason's hypothesis of countable additivity. Compactness, the source of finite configurations since Kochen and Specker, cannot reach these obstructions: in infinite dimension the finite-dimensional events admit a finitely additive noncontextual valuation, and countable additivity constrains infinitely many events at once. The tool is a theorem of infinitary real-valued logic that takes the place of compactness: every countable theory of closed conditions without models is refuted by a well-founded tree of finite contradictions, given by the winning strategies of a model existence game. Quantum probability is axiomatized by such a theory, whose standard models are exactly those of Gleason's theorem. In the strategy we construct, a hypothetical noncontextual measure is not refuted by a configuration fixed in advance: through countable additivity, the measure itself selects the four-dimensional subspace in which the measure fails. The same logic defines, in the standard models, exactly the σ-projective sets of probabilities, and whether all of them are Lebesgue measurable is independent of ZFC.
