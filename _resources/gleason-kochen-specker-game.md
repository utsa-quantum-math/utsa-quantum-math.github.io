We establish an exact axiomatic representation of quantum probability and a complete refutation theorem in the infinitary version of Keisler’s model theory for real-valued structures. The underlying structures require no prescribed metric or uniform continuity conditions; predicates have individual bounds, with no common bound imposed on their values. The standard models of our theory (those whose vector sorts are full closed unit balls of complex Hilbert spaces) represent all and only the density-operator measures on the full closed-subspace lattices of separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces.

The information about quantum probabilities depends on the logical formulation. J. L. Bell’s propositional account describes consistent definite assignments, while Hrushovski and Pitowsky’s classical first-order analysis yields quantitative constraints and effective finite approximations to quantum-state predictions. Our infinitary axioms describe the event lattice and its countably additive measure together, including normalization over infinitely many outcomes and the geometric limits defining events.

The infinitary requirements lie beyond classical compactness, which would extract a finite obstruction from an inconsistent finitary theory. We prove a complete criterion for theories axiomatized by non-strict inequalities: having no models is equivalent to admitting a well-founded refutation whose branches end in finite contradictions. The exclusion of outcome-deterministic noncontextual hidden variables holds in every model of our quantum theory, standard or otherwise. Its certificate records how countable normalization selects a finite configuration and separates geometric event identities from the requirement that their probabilities agree across contexts.

The same language defines, uniformly across standard models, exactly the σ-projective sets of probabilities in the unit interval. All these results are proved in ZFC.
