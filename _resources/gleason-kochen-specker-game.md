We use infinitary real-valued logic and model-theoretic forcing to prove a refinement of the Bell–Kochen–Specker theorem on contextuality. The classical Kochen–Specker theorem exhibits a finite graph of rays that admits no noncontextual assignment of the values 0 and 1. The refinement obtained here yields obstructing graphs in which a ray that belongs to two bases appears as two vertices, one for each basis, joined by an edge that requires them to have the same probability; the graph thereby shows exactly where the hypothesis of noncontextuality enters. Every vertex also carries an interval that bounds its probability, and the contradiction occurs in a four-dimensional subspace that the measure itself selects through σ-additivity.
