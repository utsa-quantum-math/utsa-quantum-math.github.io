We prove a refutation theorem for infinitary real-valued logic: every inconsistent theory of closed conditions is refuted by a well-founded tree of finite contradictions. The theorem yields the principal result of the paper, which concerns quantum contextuality: every obstruction to noncontextual hidden variables is finite, in infinite as in finite dimension. We axiomatize quantum probability by a theory that includes countable additivity and whose standard models represent exactly the density-operator measures on the closed subspaces of a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, the setting of Gleason’s theorem. At each hidden state, an outcome-deterministic noncontextual hidden-variable model would assign to every event the value 0 or 1, independently of the context in which the event is measured, and adding this requirement to the axioms yields an inconsistent theory, although every finite set of its axioms is consistent. Classical finite-dimensional certificates are finite configurations of rays; for this dispersion-free theory, the certificates supplied here are well-founded trees with an infinitely branching node and finitely many jointly unsatisfiable interval commitments at each terminal position. We also prove that the sets of probabilities uniformly definable across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval. All these results are proved in ZFC.
