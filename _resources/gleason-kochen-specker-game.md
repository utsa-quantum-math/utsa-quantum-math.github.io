The Kochen–Specker theorem exhibits a finite graph of rays that admits no noncontextual assignment of the values 0 and 1. We prove a refinement of this theorem for separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces in which each vertex of the obstructing graph is a ray measured in a particular basis and carries an interval that bounds its probability. Edges between the occurrences of one ray in different bases make the hypothesis of noncontextuality explicit, and the axiom of σ-additivity locates the finite block of the Hilbert space in which the contradiction arises.
