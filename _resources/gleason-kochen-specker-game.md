The classical Kochen–Specker theorem shows that a few dozen particular vectors cannot be colored 0 and 1 consistently. These notes show that this is only the finite shadow of something much stronger: in real-valued infinitary logic, where the truth value of a quantum proposition is its probability, countably many axioms already forbid two-valued truth values in **every** model, including countable models that are not Hilbert spaces at all, and in the Hilbert-space models the truth values cannot even be confined to any Borel set of values missing a point of [0,1]. The added expressive power costs nothing in concreteness: a game version of the model existence theorem still produces finite obstructions, and the classical Kochen–Specker configurations reappear among them.
