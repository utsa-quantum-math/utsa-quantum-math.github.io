We prove a refutation theorem for infinitary real-valued logic, according to which every inconsistent countable theory of closed conditions is refuted by a well-founded tree of finite contradictions. The theorem yields the principal result of the paper: every obstruction to noncontextual hidden variables in quantum mechanics is finite, including the obstructions that rest on Gleason’s hypothesis of countable additivity. The result concerns a countable axiomatization of quantum probability whose standard models represent exactly the density-operator measures on the closed subspaces of a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, the setting of Gleason’s theorem. Adjoining the dispersion-free condition, the requirement of a noncontextual assignment of the values 0 and 1 to all events, yields an inconsistent theory, although every finite set of its axioms is consistent. In our refutation of this theory, countable additivity leads the hypothetical noncontextual measure to select a four-dimensional subspace in which a parity argument yields the contradiction; the refutation records separately the geometric identities and each use of noncontextuality. We also prove that the sets of probabilities uniformly definable across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval. All these results are proved in ZFC, although ZFC does not decide whether every σ-projective set is Lebesgue measurable.
