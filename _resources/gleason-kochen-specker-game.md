We study the obstructions to noncontextual hidden variables when quantum probability is described in full, by the complete lattice of closed subspaces of a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space together with its countably additive measures. Such a description requires infinitary sentences, and we give one in infinitary real-valued logic, the infinitary extension of Keisler’s model theory of general structures: the standard models of our axioms, in which vectors and their square-summable sequences range over full closed unit balls of complex Hilbert spaces, represent exactly the density-operator measures of Gleason’s theorem. The infinitary sentences come at the price of compactness, as the hypothesis of an outcome-deterministic noncontextual model shows: the hypothesis, which at each hidden state assigns to every event the value 0 or 1 whatever the context of measurement, contradicts the axioms, yet it is consistent with every finite subset of them. The obstructions nevertheless remain finite. We prove that in this logic every inconsistent countable set of closed conditions is refuted by a well-founded tree whose branches end in finite contradictions, a tree that must branch infinitely for the hypothesis above. The resulting finiteness theorem is maximal: a hidden-variable hypothesis expressed by countably many closed conditions is inconsistent with the axioms precisely when such a tree refutes it together with them, and since the axioms represent every quantum state, every obstruction to noncontextual hidden variables is finite. The same language defines, uniformly across standard models, exactly the σ-projective sets of probabilities, and all these results are proved in ZFC.
