The Kochen–Specker theorem is a statement of two-valued propositional logic: certain finite sets of rays admit no consistent assignment of the values 0 and 1. We show that it is the shadow of a far richer phenomenon in infinitary real-valued logic, where the truth value of a quantum proposition is its probability. We give a countable theory whose standard models are, up to isomorphism, the separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces with their lattices of closed subspaces and their quantum measures, so that Gleason's theorem holds in them, and we show that quantum truth values are irreducibly continuous: in the standard models they fill the interval [0,1] and cannot be confined to any Borel set of values that misses a point of it, and in no model, countable and incomplete ones included, can they be collapsed to 0 and 1. Stating the model existence theorem as a game, we show that every hidden-variable hypothesis expressible as an axiom and incompatible with the theory is refuted by a well-founded tree of finite obstructions; for two-valuedness, we describe a strategy whose leaves are classical Kochen–Specker configurations.
