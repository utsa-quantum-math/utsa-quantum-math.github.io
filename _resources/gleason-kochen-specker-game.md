Logical compactness turns inconsistency into a finite inconsistent subtheory. Building on the model existence theorem of Aubone, Dueñez and Iovino, we prove a refutation theorem for countable theories of closed conditions in infinitary real-valued logic: a theory has no models exactly when the refuting player wins its model existence game. Every winning strategy gives a countable well-founded tree whose terminal positions contain finitely many rational interval commitments that no structure satisfies together; the committed sentences may be infinitary. Infinite branching occurs only at challenges to countable suprema and infima; finite branching recovers compactness. We apply the theorem to a countable axiomatization of quantum probability whose standard models represent density-operator measures on the closed-subspace lattice of a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space. Adjoining the dispersion-free condition gives a theory with no models, although every finite set of its axioms has a model. In an explicit winning strategy, countable additivity localizes the hypothetical dispersion-free measure to a four-dimensional parity obstruction, recording geometric identities and noncontextuality separately. We also prove that the sets of probabilities uniformly definable across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval. The refutation theorem and the classification are proved in ZFC, and the only determinacy they use is that of open games, although ZFC does not decide whether every σ-projective set is Lebesgue measurable.
