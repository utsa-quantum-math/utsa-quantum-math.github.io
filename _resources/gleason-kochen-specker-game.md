Building on the model existence theorem of Aubone, Dueñez and Iovino, we prove a refutation theorem for nonempty countable theories of closed conditions, in countable vocabularies of infinitary real-valued logic: a theory has no models exactly when the refuting player wins its model existence game. In place of the finite inconsistent subtheory that compactness supplies in first-order logic, every winning strategy gives a countable well-founded tree whose terminal positions contain finitely many rational interval commitments that no structure satisfies together; the committed sentences may be infinitary. Infinite branching occurs only at challenges to countable suprema and infima. If the tree is finitely branching, it is finite and some finite set of axioms has no models. We apply the theorem to a countable axiomatization of quantum probability whose standard models represent exactly the density-operator measures on the closed-subspace lattice of a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space. Adjoining the dispersion-free condition gives a theory with no models, standard or not, although every finite set of its axioms has a model. In an explicit winning strategy, countable additivity leads the hypothetical dispersion-free measure to select a four-dimensional subspace, in which a parity argument refutes it; the strategy records the geometric identities and the uses of noncontextuality separately. We also prove that the sets of probabilities uniformly definable across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval. The refutation theorem and the classification are proved in ZFC, with no determinacy beyond that of open games, although ZFC does not decide whether every σ-projective set is Lebesgue measurable.
