We prove a refutation theorem for infinitary real-valued logic, according to which every inconsistent countable theory of closed conditions is refuted by a well-founded tree of finite contradictions. The theorem yields the principal result of the paper, which concerns quantum contextuality: every obstruction to noncontextual hidden variables is finite, in infinite as in finite dimension. In finite dimension, Hrushovski and Pitowsky derived from Gleason’s theorem finite configurations of rays; their argument does not extend to infinite dimension, where finiteness takes another form. We axiomatize quantum probability by a countable theory whose standard models represent exactly the density-operator measures on the closed subspaces of a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, the setting of Gleason’s theorem. A noncontextual hidden-variable model would assign to every event the value 0 or 1, independently of the context in which the event is measured, and adding this requirement to the axioms yields an inconsistent theory, although every finite set of its axioms is consistent. No finite contradiction can refute such a theory; its obstructions are well-founded trees of finite contradictions, which necessarily branch infinitely. We also prove that the sets of probabilities uniformly definable across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval. All these results are proved in ZFC.
