We give a countable theory of real-valued \\( L_{\omega_1,\omega} \\) whose standard models describe a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, the lattice of its closed subspaces, and a \\( \sigma \\)-additive measure on that lattice. We show that the measures that occur in standard models are exactly those given by density operators, as in Gleason's theorem, and that in no model of the theory, standard or not, does the measure take only the values 0 and 1. Stating the model existence theorem as a game, we show that this impossibility is witnessed by a well-founded tree of finite obstructions, which branches infinitely only at the moves that concern countable suprema and infima.
