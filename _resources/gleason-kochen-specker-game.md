We prove a refutation theorem for infinitary real-valued logic: every inconsistent theory of closed conditions is refuted by a well-founded tree of finite contradictions. The theorem yields the principal result of the paper, which concerns quantum contextuality: every obstruction to noncontextual hidden variables is finite, in infinite as in finite dimension. We axiomatize quantum probability by a theory whose standard models represent exactly the density-operator measures on the closed subspaces of a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, the setting of Gleason’s theorem. Since the axioms are exact and the refutation theorem is complete, every hidden-variable hypothesis expressed by closed conditions either holds in a model of the axioms or is refuted by finite contradictions. At each hidden state, an outcome-deterministic noncontextual hidden-variable model would assign to every event the value 0 or 1, independently of the context in which the event is measured, and adding this requirement to the axioms yields an inconsistent theory, although every finite set of its axioms is consistent. No finite set of axioms refutes such a theory: its refutations are well-founded trees, each branch of which ends in finitely many commitments, on the values of sentences, that no structure satisfies together, and these trees necessarily branch infinitely. We also prove that the sets of probabilities uniformly definable across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval. All these results are proved in ZFC.
