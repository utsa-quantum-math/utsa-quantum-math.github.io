We prove a refutation theorem for infinitary real-valued logic, the infinitary extension of Keisler’s model theory of general structures. Every inconsistent theory of closed conditions in this logic is refuted by a well-founded tree of finite contradictions. The theorem yields the principal result of the paper, which concerns quantum contextuality: every obstruction to noncontextual hidden variables is finite, in infinite as in finite dimension. We axiomatize quantum probability in the setting of Gleason’s theorem. We call a model standard when its vectors and square-summable sequences of vectors range over full closed unit balls of complex Hilbert spaces. The standard models of our theory represent exactly the density-operator measures on the closed subspaces of separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces. The principal result is maximal in a precise sense. Every density-operator measure is represented by a standard model, and a hidden-variable hypothesis given by countably many closed conditions is inconsistent with the axioms if and only if finite contradictions refute it. The finiteness resides in the contradictions and cannot be transferred to the axioms. An outcome-deterministic noncontextual model assigns to every event the value 0 or 1 at each hidden state, whatever the context in which the event is measured. The requirement makes the axioms inconsistent, yet every finite set of the resulting axioms is consistent. Every refutation of the resulting theory therefore branches infinitely. We also prove that the sets of probabilities uniformly definable across standard models are exactly the σ-projective subsets of the unit interval. All these results are proved in ZFC.
