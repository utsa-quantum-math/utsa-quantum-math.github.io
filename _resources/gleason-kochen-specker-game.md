We use infinitary real-valued logic and model-theoretic forcing to prove a refinement of the Bell–Kochen–Specker theorem on contextuality. The original Kochen–Specker theorem exhibits a finite graph of rays, each ray a single vertex in all the orthonormal bases that contain it, that admits no assignment of the values 0 and 1 giving the value 1 to exactly one ray of each basis and to no two orthogonal rays. The refinement obtained here yields obstructing graphs in which a ray that belongs to two bases appears as two vertices, one for each basis, joined by an explicit identification; the graph thereby shows exactly which axioms identify the two occurrences of a ray, and which clause carries the probability across the identification. Every vertex also carries an interval that bounds its probability. The graphs are built from rays, for which σ-additivity is indispensable in infinite dimension, and the contradiction occurs in a four-dimensional subspace that the hypothetical measure itself locates.
