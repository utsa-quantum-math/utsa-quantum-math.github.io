We axiomatize quantum probability to explain how the exclusion of outcome-deterministic noncontextual hidden variables depends on its geometric and probabilistic assumptions. The passage from J. L. Bell’s propositional formulation to Hrushovski and Pitowsky’s classical first-order analysis carries the argument from the impossibility of definite assignments to quantitative constraints on probabilities, including finite approximations to quantum-state predictions. We continue this analysis in the infinitary version of Keisler’s model theory for real-valued structures, where the normalization of measurements with infinitely many possible outcomes and the geometric limits defining quantum events can be retained within the refutation. The framework prescribes no metric, uniform continuity conditions or common bound on predicate values. The representation theorem is exact: the standard models of our theory (those whose vector sorts are full closed unit balls of complex Hilbert spaces) represent all and only the density-operator measures on the closed subspaces of separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces.

Countable additivity then leads a hypothetical assignment of definite outcomes to a finite configuration on which it fails. The certificate records how normalization over infinitely many outcomes makes this obstruction applicable and separates the geometric identification of events across measurement contexts from the requirement that their probabilities agree. The infinitary assumptions preclude the compactness argument available in a finitary setting, but admit a complete refutation criterion: a theory axiomatized by non-strict inequalities has no models exactly when it admits a well-founded refutation whose branches end in finite contradictions. These certificates preserve the reasoning from the geometric and probabilistic assumptions to each obstruction.

The same language defines, uniformly across standard models, exactly the σ-projective sets of probabilities in the unit interval. All these results are proved in ZFC.
