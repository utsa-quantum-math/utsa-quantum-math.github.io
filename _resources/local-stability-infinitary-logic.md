This paper extends local stability to the logic L<sub>ω₁ω</sub>(ℝ), the infinitary extension of Keisler's model theory of real-valued structures, whose expressive power places it beyond the reach of the compactness theorem. Without compactness, the types realized in elementary extensions take the place of all types. In this form, the four classical characterizations of stability extend to L<sub>ω₁ω</sub>(ℝ): the absence of the order property, the commutation of iterated limits, the separately continuous extension to types, and the definability of types. The definitions are explicit and canonical, since the upper limit of the instances along any sequence of elements approximating a realized type defines that type, with no assumption of separability. Because the realized types may form a non-Borel set, the proof rests on a double limit criterion for arbitrary prescribed sets of limits, which we establish in the setting of Arhangel'skii's Cₚ-theory. Model existence, which Aubone, Dueñez and Iovino derived from the Čech-completeness of the spaces of Henkin types, presents the realized types as analytic sets for relational vocabularies, and every analytic set of binary sequences containing the finitely supported ones arises in this way from a stable formula. Finally, stationary finite approximations recover the generic value of a stable formula even for types that have no joint realization, or none with the generic value; the second phenomenon occurs even when the local type spaces are compact.
