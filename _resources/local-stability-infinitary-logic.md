We prove that stable formulas of infinitary real-valued logic, the infinitary extension of Keisler's model theory of real-valued structures, have canonical definitions of their local types over countable models, although the compactness theorem fails for this logic and some limits of types are not realized. For a fixed countable fragment, the upper limit of the instances along any sequence of elements approximating a realized type defines that type, and every such sequence induces the same continuous function on the realized dual types; stability is equivalent to this definability, to the absence of the order property with realized limits, and to separately continuous extension to the realized types. For a bounded function on a product of two countable sets, and arbitrary subsets of the pointwise closures of its rows and of its columns that contain the rows and the columns, we prove that the exchange of iterated limits along the prescribed subsets is equivalent to separately continuous extension to their product and to the absence of a ladder converging to a pair in it. The realized local types of a stable formula over a countable model can form a non-Borel set: every analytic set of binary sequences containing the sequences with only finitely many coordinates equal to 1 arises in this way. Even when both local type spaces are compact and a realized type and a realized dual type are realized together, every joint realization can give a value different from the canonical one. Stationary finite approximations by elements of the model characterize stability and recover the canonical value, also for pairs of types that have no joint realization.
