The classical characterizations of stability rest on the compactness theorem. We show that, for local stability over countable models, they extend to the infinitary extension of Keisler's model theory of real-valued structures, a logic without equality, without moduli of uniform continuity, and beyond the reach of compactness: the four classical characterizations remain equivalent when they are read on the types realized in elementary extensions, and every realized type of a stable formula is defined by an explicit formula of the logic. Two phenomena, neither of which can occur in first-order logic, show that the extended theory is not a copy of the classical one. The realized types of a stable formula may form a non-Borel set: every analytic set of binary sequences that contains the sequences with only finitely many coordinates equal to 1 arises in this way. And two realized types of a stable formula may never be realized together, even when the iterated limits of the formula commute on all limits of types.
