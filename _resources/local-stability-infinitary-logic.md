We prove that stable formulas of infinitary real-valued logic, the infinitary extension of Keisler's model theory of real-valued structures, have canonical definitions of their realized local types over countable models, although the compactness theorem fails for this logic and some limits of types are not realized. For a fixed countable fragment, the values of the instances along any sequence of elements approximating a realized type on the model converge at every realized dual type, to a limit that does not depend on the sequence and is continuous on the realized dual types, and the upper-limit formula of the instances defines that limit; stability is equivalent to this definability and to the absence of the order property with realized limits. These definitions exist even when the type admits no uniform approximation by finite combinations of instances. For a bounded function on a product of two countable sets, and arbitrary subsets of the pointwise closures of its rows and of its columns that contain the rows and the columns, we prove that the exchange of iterated limits along the prescribed subsets is equivalent to the existence of a separately continuous extension to their product, which is then unique, and to the absence of a ladder whose two limits belong to the prescribed subsets. The realized local types of a stable formula over a countable model can form a non-Borel set: every analytic set of binary sequences containing the sequences with only finitely many coordinates equal to 1 arises in this way. Even when both local type spaces are compact, a jointly realized pair of types can have no joint realization with its canonical value. A characterization of stability by free finite approximations in the base model recovers that value, also for pairs of types that have no joint realization.
