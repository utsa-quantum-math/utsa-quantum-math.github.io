We prove that stable formulas of infinitary real-valued logic, the infinitary extension of Keisler's model theory of real-valued structures, have canonical definitions of their realized local types over countable models, although the compactness theorem fails for this logic and some limits of types are not realized. Fix a countable fragment. Along any sequence of elements approximating a realized type on the model, the values of the instances converge at every realized dual type; the resulting function does not depend on the sequence and is continuous on the realized dual types, and the upper-limit formula of the instances defines it, even when uniform approximation by finite combinations of instances fails. Stability is equivalent to definability by such formulas, compatible with limits, and to the absence of the order property with realized limits. The proof rests on a double limit criterion for bounded functions on products of two countable sets: for arbitrary subsets of the pointwise closures of the rows and of the columns that contain the rows and the columns, the exchange of iterated limits along the prescribed subsets is equivalent to the existence of a separately continuous extension to their product, which is then unique, and to the absence of a ladder whose two limits belong to the prescribed subsets. The realized local types of a stable formula over a countable model can form a non-Borel set: every analytic set of binary sequences containing the sequences with only finitely many coordinates equal to 1 arises in this way. Even when both local type spaces are compact and every local limit is realized, a jointly realized pair of types can have no joint realization with its canonical value. A characterization of stability by free finite approximations in the base model recovers that value, also for pairs of types that have no joint realization.
