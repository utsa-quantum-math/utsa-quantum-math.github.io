This paper extends local stability over countable models to the logic L<sub>ω₁ω</sub>(ℝ), the infinitary extension of Keisler's model theory of real-valued structures, whose expressive power places it beyond the reach of the compactness theorem. Without compactness, the types realized in elementary extensions take the place of all types. In this form, the four classical characterizations of stability extend to L<sub>ω₁ω</sub>(ℝ): the absence of the order property with realized limits of types, the commutation of iterated limits, the separately continuous extension to realized types, and the definability of realized types by formulas that induce continuous functions on the realized types and are compatible with limits. The definitions are explicit and canonical, since the upper limit of the instances along any sequence of elements approximating a realized type defines that type, with no assumption of separability for the uniform metric. Because the realized types may form a non-Borel set, the proof rests on a double limit criterion for arbitrary prescribed sets of limits, which we establish in the setting of Arhangel'skii's Cₚ-theory. Model existence, which Aubone, Dueñez and Iovino derived from the Čech-completeness of the spaces of Henkin types, presents the realized types as analytic sets for relational vocabularies, and every analytic set of binary sequences containing those that take the value 1 at only finitely many coordinates arises in this way from a stable formula. Finally, stability is characterized by stationary finite approximations, which recover the generic value even for types that have no joint realization, or none with the generic value; the second phenomenon occurs even when the local type spaces are compact.
