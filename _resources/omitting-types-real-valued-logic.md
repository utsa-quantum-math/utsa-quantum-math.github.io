For countable vocabularies, we extend the omitting types theorem and Scott’s theorem to the infinitary logic of Keisler’s general real-valued structures, without equality, a prescribed metric or moduli of continuity. Each formula has its own finite bound; no common bound for the whole language is required. For every countable fragment, the Henkin types form a Polish, Čech-complete space, and under the omitting-types hypothesis the types omitting the prescribed countable family form a dense Gδ subset of the relative space satisfying the theory. A single Scott sentence characterizes every structure with countable sorts, among all structures, up to approximate back-and-forth equivalence, equivalently agreement on all sentence values. On the space of structures with universe the natural numbers, surjections acting on atomic diagrams replace permutations: every approximate-equivalence class is Borel and is separated from the other classes by a Borel Lipschitz function. The bounded Borel functions invariant under exact equivalence are precisely sentence values in the logic enlarged by continuous connectives. For finite predicate ranges with a uniform normalized gap, the representation holds in the original syntax, yielding López-Escobar’s theorem for classical infinitary logic without equality.
