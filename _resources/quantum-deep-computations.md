## Abstract

We show that adding a single probe can make the compactum of pointwise limits of a fixed family of quantum channels first countable. Forgetting the added probe collapses a Cantor set of limits to one point. Restricted to the records of density operators, the limits always form a metrizable compactum, so that whenever the compactum of limits is not metrizable, two distinct limits agree at every density operator; for this family, with both probes retained, the restriction identifies explicit pairs of limits.

A channel need not act on the records of the original probe expectations. Closing the observations under the channels gives the coarsest refinement of observational equivalence that the channels respect. On the completed records each channel acts continuously. With countably many channels and probes, all limits on each invariant energy shard are of the first Baire class exactly when, at each fixed output coordinate, the responses on actual preparations have no independent sequence as the computation varies. This puts the compacta of limits within Todorčević's trichotomy.

The same quantum walk has only continuous limiting maps with site probes and a discontinuous limit with position comparisons; in both cases the compactum of limits is metrizable. Measure-and-prepare channels on a binary tree realize all three nonmetrizable alternatives, with exact identifications of specified indexed families.
