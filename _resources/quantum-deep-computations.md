## Abstract

We show that the topology of the limits of a family of quantum channels depends on the observations through which the channels are seen, even when the channels themselves are fixed. The records of chosen expectations need not determine what the same observations report after a channel acts. Closing the observations under the channels gives the coarsest refinement of observational equivalence that the channels respect, and on the completed records every channel acts continuously. The classification of classical deep computations by Todorčević's trichotomy thereby extends to quantum channels: for countably many channels and observations, on invariant sets of preparations, all limits are of the first Baire class exactly when the responses at each fixed output coordinate have no independent sequence.

The same quantum walk on the integers has only continuous limits when observed at individual sites, and a discontinuous limit, in a metrizable compactum, when its position is compared with a fixed site. Measure-and-prepare channels on the binary tree realize all three nonmetrizable alternatives of the trichotomy through explicit canonical families. For one of these families, a single additional probe makes the compactum of limits first countable, and forgetting that probe collapses a Cantor set of limits to a point. Some distinct limits agree at every density operator and are separated only by idealized records.
