The Stone–Weierstrass theorem obtains every continuous function on a compact space as a uniform limit of functions built from a separating family by finitely many operations. We extend the theorem to every separable metric space, and in particular to every Polish space, by admitting countable suprema and infima according to weights computed from the construction. For a countable, uniformly bounded separating family whose supremum metric makes the space separable, the functions so built are exactly the bounded Lipschitz functions, and their uniform limits are exactly the bounded uniformly continuous functions. The theorem was discovered, and first proved, in infinitary real-valued logic, where the weights are the a priori bounds of formulas and the uniform approximation is equivalent to an approximate form of Beth's definability theorem, which carries Reggio's correspondence from compact spaces to separable metric spaces. The same bounds draw an exact line between the two forms of definability when the implicit definition has a separable space of models: an implicitly defined predicate has an explicit definition precisely when the defining function is Lipschitz, and admits uniform approximation by sentences precisely when that function is uniformly continuous. We also give a purely topological-analytic proof of the theorem.
