We show that two games on the space of types of a separable Banach space decide stability and independence: Player II wins the commitment game for every type exactly when the space is stable, and wins the revision game, which at tolerance zero is Kiss’s game, for every type exactly when the space has no independent sequence of positive gap. Both characterizations are quantitative: a ladder or an independent sequence of gap δ gives Player I a winning strategy at every tolerance below an explicit multiple of δ. Tsirelson’s space separates the two games. Among block subspaces, we prove that every space with a basis has a stable block subspace or a block subspace in which every block subspace carries infinite ladders of one gap, with parameters from one finite-dimensional space; without the parameters, such a gap would exclude, with a margin, every subspace close to a stable space.
