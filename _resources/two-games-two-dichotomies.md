We show that two games on the space of types of a separable Banach space decide stability and independence, each with a quantitative margin. Player II wins the commitment game for every type exactly when the space is stable, and wins the revision game, which at tolerance zero is Kiss's game, for every type exactly when the space has no independent sequence of positive gap. An infinite ladder of gap δ places some type at uniform distance at least δ/2 from every polynomial in finitely many norms, and gives Player I a winning strategy at every tolerance below δ; an independent sequence of gap δ gives Player I a winning strategy at every tolerance below δ/4, and makes the types act as 2<sup>𝔠</sup> distinct functions. A uniform gap for infinite ladders in every block subspace excludes, with a margin, every infinite-dimensional subspace close to a stable space. Every space with a basis has a stable block subspace or a block subspace with such a uniform gap, with parameters confined to one finite-dimensional space.
