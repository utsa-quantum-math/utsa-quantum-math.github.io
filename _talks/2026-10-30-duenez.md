Quantum error correction addresses a problem with no classical counterpart: protecting an unknown quantum state from noise, when measuring the state disturbs it and the possible errors form a continuum.
The resolution, found in the mid-1990s, is algebraic.
Suitably chosen commuting measurements produce a binary *syndrome* that identifies the error without revealing the encoded state, and correcting finitely many errors turns out to correct all of their linear combinations.
This talk introduces these ideas from a mathematical point of view, organized around stabilizer codes.
(Part of a series leading to surface codes and to decoding, which is at heart a problem of statistical inference.)
Only undergraduate linear algebra and elementary probability are assumed; no background in quantum mechanics is required.
