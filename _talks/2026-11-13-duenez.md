Quantum error correction addresses a problem with no classical counterpart: protecting an unknown quantum state from noise, when measuring the state disturbs it and the possible errors form a continuum.
The resolution, found in the mid-1990s, is algebraic.
Suitably chosen commuting measurements produce a binary *syndrome* that reveals check violations without revealing the encoded state, and correcting finitely many errors turns out to correct all of their linear combinations.
This fourth and final lecture gives a rapid review of the basic formalism, followed by a visual survey of stabilizer codes, surface codes, and decoding through worked examples.
Only undergraduate linear algebra and elementary probability are assumed; no background in quantum mechanics is required.

Slides and notes are linked above. An [interactive nine-qubit patch](/assets/notes/qec-finale/patch-demo.html) lets you toggle errors, inspect their syndromes, and compare successful corrections with logical failures. It runs in a browser without installations.
