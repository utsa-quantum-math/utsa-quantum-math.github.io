From infinitary inconsistency to finite obstruction.

The Kochen–Specker theorem says that a certain finite configuration cannot be coloured. Our refinement concerns the logical form of the obstruction. An infinitary theory with no model is refuted through a well-founded game, and every terminal position keeps a finite set of commitments together with the dependencies that forced them. The familiar parity argument is one component of one particular strategy.

01

Infinitely branching. Every branch ends.

The theory T01 (a probability measure on the projection lattice of a Hilbert space that takes only the values 0 and 1) has no model, yet every finite set of its axioms has one. Olivia's winning strategy is fixed, so the tree of plays branches only at Josh's answers. At the countable challenge he may name any N, and the tree fans out infinitely there; the plays get longer as N grows. No branch is infinite. Every play stops at a leaf, a finite position that no structure satisfies.

Purple edges: Josh's answers. Pink diamonds: Olivia's challenges. Pink dots: leaves, where Josh has lost. Hover or tap a leaf.
02

Play it.

Answer for Josh. Every answer is legal, and every answer loses: a different N gives a different leaf, sometimes in a different four-dimensional block. What the play leaves behind is the finite obstruction.

03

A leaf keeps its dependencies.

One leaf, opened up. Each commitment of the final position is shown with what forced it: the countable answer, the instances of the axioms, the geometric identifications and the interval commitments, down to one basic sentence that has received intervals with no common point. Hover over a node to light up everything it depends on. The parity count is one bracketed module of this derivation.

04

The parity module.

In this strategy, the last commitments of every leaf concern nine bases of four rays inside the selected block, in the configuration of Cabello, Estebaranz and García-Alcaine. The leaf records each ray once in each of its two bases, together with an explicit identification derived from axioms that do not mention the measure. Choose a ray and omit its identification: the remaining constraints become satisfiable, so the contradiction uses every one of the eighteen.

The count tolerates error. If each value lies within δ of 0 or 1 and each basis sum lies within η of 1, it still refutes the commitments whenever 4δ + η < 1, and this holds for the ordinary ray presentation as well. What the leaf adds is where the intervals come from: they are Josh's explicit commitments, each with the challenge that forced it.

The same separation of occurrences and identifications holds for the 49-ray set found by our search: all 92 occurrences of its shared rays are indispensable.
