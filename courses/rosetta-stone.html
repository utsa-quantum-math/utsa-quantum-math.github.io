The configuration behind the connections

Three tests. Eight prescriptions.

Each column below represents a witness realizing one prescription of high and low values on three tests. All eight prescriptions occur. For an infinite independent sequence of tests, every prescription on any finite selection must have a witness.

Combinatorics
The tests ask for membership in three sets. The witnesses realize every membership pattern.
Analysis
The tests are real functions. At each witness, their values lie below a fixed low threshold or above a fixed high threshold.
Dynamics
The witness is an initial state. One observation of its orbit meets the prescribed low or high test at each selected time.

The starting point

A limit can lose continuity without losing structure.

On the interval [0, 1], each function fn(x) = xn is continuous. Its values tend to zero at every input below 1, while the value at 1 remains 1. The limit is discontinuous.

It is nevertheless a function of the first Baire class: a pointwise limit of continuous functions. We begin with this distinction and then study when an entire pointwise closure retains such regularity. The restriction on independent patterns that emerges will accompany us into other subjects.

A common language

The role of model theory.

The independence property was isolated by Shelah in model theory. Its absence, abbreviated NIP, connects the configurations studied throughout the course. We develop the mathematics in the language of each subject before returning to the logical interpretation.

Previous study of model theory is not required for the main development. Optional readings make the logical connections precise for students familiar with compactness, types, and saturation.
